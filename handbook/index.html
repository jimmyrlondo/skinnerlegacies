Berea Office · 398 W. Bagley Rd., Suites 9 & 10, Berea, OH 44017

Welcome

This handbook explains what you can expect from Skinner Legacies, your rights and responsibilities, program expectations, safety procedures, confidentiality practices, and how to ask questions, provide feedback, or raise concerns. This handbook is provided as part of client orientation. Staff will review it with you and answer questions.

Welcome & How to Use This Handbook

Welcome to Skinner Legacies. Our goal is to provide respectful, person-centered behavioral health services that recognize the whole person. We want you and your family or support system, when appropriate, to understand your services and to participate actively in decisions about care.

Staff will review this handbook with you during orientation and answer questions. You may ask for information to be explained in a different way, in accessible language, or with reasonable accommodations. This handbook does not replace individualized clinical recommendations, your treatment plan, or notices required by law.

Important

If you are experiencing an immediate medical, fire, safety, or life-threatening emergency, call 911. If you or someone else is experiencing a mental health or suicide crisis, call or text 988.

1. About Skinner Legacies and Services

Skinner Legacies is a behavioral health organization serving adults, children, adolescents, and families. Services are provided based on individual need, eligibility, clinical appropriateness, payer requirements, and program availability.

Services May Include

  • Behavioral health assessment and psychotherapy/therapy.
  • Community Psychiatric Supportive Treatment (CPST) and case management.
  • Therapeutic Behavioral Services (TBS) and Psychosocial Rehabilitation (PSR), when available and clinically appropriate.
  • OhioRISE-related services, including CANS-related activities and Behavioral Health Respite when authorized and available.
  • Crisis intervention services within the scope of the agency.
  • Mental Health Day Treatment and structured group-based programming.
  • Care coordination, referrals, linkage to community resources, and collaboration with outside providers when authorized.

Not every service is appropriate or available for every person. Your care team will explain the services recommended for you, the expected frequency and duration, and any authorization or eligibility requirements.

Our Approach

Skinner Legacies emphasizes dignity, equality, cultural responsiveness, recovery, resilience, choice, family involvement when appropriate, and services that build on each person's strengths. Services should be provided in the least restrictive, most appropriate setting consistent with safety and clinical need.

2. Person-Centered Philosophy and Participation in Care

You are an active partner in your care. Person-centered services begin with your goals, strengths, preferences, culture, needs, abilities, risks, and desired outcomes. Your treatment team will work with you to develop and update your individualized plan.

  • Ask questions and receive understandable information about your care.
  • Participate in setting goals and choosing strategies that are clinically appropriate.
  • Identify people you want involved in your care, subject to consent and legal requirements.
  • Discuss what is working, what is not working, and what you would like changed.
  • Receive information about alternatives, referrals, and transitions when a service is not appropriate or available.

3. Client Rights

Skinner Legacies respects the legal, civil, human, and treatment rights of every person served. Rights are protected without retaliation for exercising them.

  • Be treated with consideration, respect, dignity, privacy, and freedom from discrimination.
  • Receive services in a safe, humane, and professional environment.
  • Receive information about services, staff roles, program expectations, fees, and available alternatives.
  • Participate in treatment planning and receive information needed to make informed decisions.
  • Refuse a service or withdraw consent when legally permitted, after being informed of reasonably foreseeable consequences.
  • Receive services free from abuse, neglect, exploitation, humiliation, retaliation, coercion, and unnecessary restriction.
  • Be free from seclusion and restraint by Skinner Legacies. Skinner Legacies does not use seclusion or restraint as a treatment intervention.
  • Have personal information kept confidential as required by law.
  • Request access to or amendment of records as permitted by law and agency policy.
  • Receive information about how to file a complaint, grievance, appeal, or request a Medicaid fair hearing when applicable.
  • Communicate concerns and provide feedback without interference or retaliation.
  • Request reasonable accommodations and communication assistance.
  • Receive services that consider cultural, linguistic, spiritual, accessibility, and other individual needs.
  • Be informed of significant changes in services, program rules, or your treatment plan.
  • Receive assistance with transition, discharge, and referrals when appropriate.

No Retaliation

You will not be denied appropriate services, punished, threatened, or treated differently because you ask questions, make a complaint, file a grievance or appeal, request an accommodation, or exercise a protected right.

4. Client Responsibilities

  • Participate honestly and actively in assessment, treatment planning, and services.
  • Provide accurate information about health, medications, symptoms, risks, insurance, contact information, and other matters relevant to care.
  • Treat other clients, visitors, and personnel respectfully.
  • Follow reasonable safety rules and staff instructions during emergencies.
  • Keep scheduled appointments or notify the agency as soon as possible when you cannot attend.
  • Protect the privacy of other clients. Do not photograph, record, post, or share another client's information without permission.
  • Do not bring weapons, illegal substances, alcohol, or prohibited items onto agency property.
  • Tell staff promptly about safety concerns, threats, medical emergencies, suicidal thoughts, abuse, neglect, or other urgent risks.
  • Ask questions when you do not understand information or instructions.
  • Meet financial obligations that have been explained and agreed upon, when applicable.

5. Confidentiality, Privacy, and Information Sharing

Skinner Legacies protects your health information in accordance with applicable privacy and confidentiality laws. Information is accessed only by authorized individuals who need it for treatment, payment, health care operations, legal requirements, or another permitted purpose.

When Your Permission Is Generally Needed

When authorization is required, Skinner Legacies will ask you or your legally authorized representative to sign an Authorization for Release of Information identifying what may be shared, with whom, for what purpose, and for how long. Substance use disorder records may receive additional protections under federal law.

When Information May Be Shared Without Your Authorization

The law permits or requires certain disclosures without written authorization. Examples may include reporting suspected child abuse or neglect; reporting certain abuse, neglect, or exploitation of vulnerable adults; responding to a serious and imminent safety threat when legally permitted; medical emergencies; certain court orders or legal requirements; health oversight activities; and other disclosures specifically permitted or required by law.

Emergency Privacy

In a genuine emergency, staff may share the minimum information reasonably necessary with emergency responders or other appropriate persons when the law permits the disclosure to protect health or safety.

Privacy in Group Services

Staff are required to protect confidentiality. Clients participating in groups are also expected to respect the privacy of others. Skinner Legacies cannot guarantee that another participant will maintain confidentiality outside the group, so clients should carefully consider what personal information they choose to share.

6. Consent to Treatment and Participation

Before services begin, Skinner Legacies obtains appropriate consent for treatment and explains the nature and purpose of services, reasonably expected benefits and risks, available alternatives, privacy practices, financial responsibilities, and other information needed for informed participation.

  • Consent must be voluntary unless services are required under a lawful order or another legal exception applies.
  • Parents, guardians, custodians, or other legally authorized representatives may be involved when required or permitted by law.
  • Consent may be revisited when services materially change.
  • You may ask questions before signing any consent or authorization.
  • Refusing or withdrawing consent may affect the services Skinner Legacies can provide; staff will explain foreseeable consequences and available alternatives.

7. Behavioral Expectations and Program Rules

Program expectations exist to protect the safety, privacy, dignity, and therapeutic environment of everyone in the facility.

  • No violence, threats, intimidation, harassment, bullying, or deliberate destruction of property.
  • No weapons or items being used or threatened as weapons.
  • No alcohol, illegal drugs, unauthorized controlled substances, or misuse of legal substances on agency property.
  • No smoking, vaping, or other nicotine use inside the facility. Follow posted property rules regarding nicotine use.
  • No photographing, video recording, audio recording, livestreaming, or posting other clients without authorization.
  • Respect staff directions related to safety, emergencies, privacy, and program operations.
  • Maintain appropriate clothing and behavior for a professional treatment environment.
  • Do not engage in sexual activity, sexual harassment, or sexually inappropriate conduct on agency property.
  • Do not disrupt services in a way that prevents others from safely participating.

Restrictions and Consequences

Skinner Legacies uses the least restrictive response reasonably necessary. Depending on the behavior and level of risk, staff may redirect behavior, develop a safety plan, temporarily restrict access to a particular activity or area, contact a parent/guardian when appropriate, arrange clinical review, pause a service, require a meeting before return, or initiate discharge/transfer when continued participation cannot be provided safely.

Regaining Restricted Privileges

When a privilege or activity is temporarily restricted, staff will explain the reason, what must occur for the restriction to be reconsidered, and how the client can demonstrate safe participation. Restrictions are reviewed based on safety, clinical need, behavior, and progress and are not used as punishment or retaliation.

8. Health, Safety, and Prohibited Items

  • Seclusion and restraint are not used as treatment interventions by Skinner Legacies.
  • Nicotine products may not be used inside the facility.
  • Illegal drugs, alcohol, drug paraphernalia, and substances being misused are prohibited.
  • Weapons are prohibited on agency premises, subject to applicable law and emergency/law-enforcement circumstances.
  • Clients should immediately tell staff about spills, exposed wires, water leaks, tripping hazards, damaged equipment, unsafe behavior, or other environmental hazards.
  • Staff may contact emergency services or law enforcement when necessary to address an immediate safety threat.

9. Medications Brought to the Program

Skinner Legacies is not a medication storage or dispensing site unless a specific program has an approved process stating otherwise. Clients should keep medications secured and under their own control or the control of an authorized caregiver.

  • Bring only medications needed for your personal use and keep them in the original labeled container whenever possible.
  • Do not share, sell, trade, or give medications to another person.
  • Tell staff promptly if a medication creates an urgent health concern or if accidental ingestion, overdose, or medication misuse is suspected.
  • Emergency medical services will be contacted when a medication-related event may be life-threatening.
  • Program-specific medication procedures, if applicable, will be explained separately.

10. Emergency, Crisis, and After-Hours Services

Skinner Legacies is not an emergency department and does not replace 911, 988, or hospital emergency services. Staff will assist with crisis response within the agency's scope and will connect clients to higher levels of care when needed.

11. Severe Weather, Natural Disasters, and Overnight Emergencies

Skinner Legacies maintains procedures for severe weather and other emergencies, including tornadoes, severe thunderstorms, high winds, winter weather, flooding, utility failures, fire, medical emergencies, bomb threats, and violent or threatening situations.

Severe Weather Shelter-in-Place

At the Berea office, the designated severe weather shelter areas are:

  • Office #1
  • Office #2
  • Bathroom

These rooms do not have windows. During a severe-weather warning or other event requiring shelter-in-place, staff will direct clients, visitors, and personnel away from the Lobby and Group Room windows/exterior doors and into the designated shelter areas. Everyone should remain there until the warning ends or emergency officials indicate that it is safe to leave.

ReadyNotify

Cuyahoga County's ReadyNotify system provides emergency notifications, including weather alerts and other safety information. Skinner Legacies may use ReadyNotify and other official emergency alerts to monitor conditions and instructions affecting the Berea office. Clients and families may also voluntarily register for ReadyNotify through Cuyahoga County.

Extended or Overnight Emergency

If a severe-weather or community emergency extends beyond normal business hours, clients and personnel may remain sheltered at Skinner Legacies, including overnight when necessary, when the building remains safe and sheltering in place is appropriate. Staff will continue to monitor official instructions and address basic safety needs to the extent reasonably possible.

If the building becomes unsafe, loses essential utilities for an extended period, or authorities order evacuation, Skinner Legacies will follow emergency instructions and coordinate relocation. Call 911 for immediate life-safety emergencies. For emergency shelter or community-resource coordination, staff may contact 211. A specific external shelter is not guaranteed because locations and availability may change during an emergency.

Emergency Supplies

  • First-aid supplies
  • Flashlights and available battery/charging capability
  • Bottled water and basic emergency supplies
  • Emergency contact information
  • Blankets or other basic comfort items when available
  • Communication devices for emergency alerts and calls

12. Abuse, Neglect, Exploitation, and Other Safety Risks

Skinner Legacies personnel follow applicable mandatory-reporting and safety requirements. If staff learn of suspected abuse, neglect, exploitation, sexual assault, serious self-harm risk, overdose, violence, or another critical safety concern, staff may be required to take protective action and make reports to appropriate authorities.

Whenever possible and legally appropriate, staff will explain what action is being taken and why. Confidentiality does not prevent reports that are required by law or disclosures that are legally permitted to address a serious safety concern.

13. Complaints, Grievances, Appeals, and Medicaid Due Process

You have the right to raise a concern about services, personnel, access, treatment decisions, program rules, privacy, discrimination, safety, billing, or any other aspect of your experience.

How to Raise a Concern

  • Speak with the staff member involved, when comfortable.
  • Ask to speak with the Clinical Director or Chief Operating Officer.
  • Submit a written grievance using the Skinner Legacies grievance form or another written format.
  • Ask for assistance writing or communicating your grievance.
  • Use external complaint, appeal, or fair-hearing processes available through your payer, regulatory agency, or law.

Skinner Legacies will review grievances fairly, document the concern and response, communicate the outcome as required, and protect clients from retaliation. See our Grievance Policy and Procedure for the complete process and timelines.

Appeals and Medicaid Fair Hearing Rights

If a health plan or Medicaid entity denies, reduces, suspends, or terminates a covered service, you may have appeal and/or state fair-hearing rights. Deadlines can apply. Any notice you receive should explain the action, the reason, the deadline, and how to appeal or request a hearing. Skinner Legacies can help you understand where to direct questions but cannot guarantee the outcome of an appeal or hearing.

14. Client Input, Feedback, and Satisfaction

Your feedback helps Skinner Legacies improve services. You may provide input through conversations with staff, satisfaction surveys, grievances, treatment-plan reviews, suggestion/feedback opportunities, stakeholder activities, or other methods offered by the agency.

  • Feedback may be positive, negative, or suggest a change.
  • You may ask for help communicating feedback.
  • Providing feedback will not affect your right to appropriate services.
  • Skinner Legacies reviews feedback for trends and opportunities to improve quality, access, safety, and client experience.

15. Financial Responsibilities and Good Faith Estimates

Before services, Skinner Legacies will explain known financial responsibilities to the extent reasonably possible. Depending on coverage and service type, this may include insurance billing, copayments, deductibles, coinsurance, self-pay charges, or services that are not covered.

Uninsured or self-pay clients may receive a Good Faith Estimate when required. The estimate is based on information available at the time and is not a guarantee that circumstances, clinical needs, or the course of treatment will never change. See our Cost of Services & Good Faith Estimate for current rates and policy.

  • Provide accurate and current insurance information.
  • Tell the office promptly if insurance coverage changes.
  • Ask questions about charges or estimates before services when possible.
  • Review financial notices and agreements provided by the agency.
  • Contact the office if you believe a bill or financial notice is incorrect.

16. Attendance, Scheduling, and Communication

Consistent participation supports continuity and progress. Appointment expectations may differ by program.

  • Arrive on time and be prepared to participate.
  • Notify Skinner Legacies as soon as possible if you need to cancel or reschedule.
  • Repeated missed appointments may result in outreach, treatment-plan review, service modification, transition, or discharge when clinically and administratively appropriate.
  • Keep your telephone number, address, emergency contact, guardian information, and insurance information current.
  • Do not use ordinary text, email, or social media for emergencies.

17. Transition, Discharge, and Continuity of Care

Transition and discharge planning are part of treatment. Services may end or change when goals are achieved, the client no longer meets medical necessity or program criteria, another level of care is needed, the client requests discharge, eligibility/authorization ends, the client relocates, participation cannot be safely maintained, or another clinically appropriate reason applies.

When Services End

When possible, Skinner Legacies will involve you in planning, review progress and remaining needs, provide recommendations or referrals, explain follow-up steps, and coordinate with other providers when authorized. Emergency or urgent safety needs may require an immediate transfer to a higher level of care.

A discharge does not prevent you from requesting services again in the future, subject to clinical appropriateness, program availability, eligibility, and payer requirements.

18. Mandated or Court-Involved Services

Some clients may participate because of a court order, probation/parole requirement, child welfare plan, school requirement, employer requirement, or another external mandate. Skinner Legacies will explain known reporting requirements and the limits of confidentiality that apply to the referral.

Ending services does not necessarily end obligations imposed by a court, probation department, child welfare agency, employer, school, or other referring authority. Required reports or follow-up may still occur after discharge when authorized or required by law, consent, or the applicable mandate.

19. Professional Conduct and Boundaries

Skinner Legacies personnel are expected to maintain professional, ethical, and appropriate boundaries. Staff should not exploit clients financially, emotionally, sexually, socially, or otherwise.

  • Staff-client relationships must remain professional.
  • Staff may not engage in sexual or romantic relationships with current clients.
  • Staff should avoid conflicts of interest, coercion, favoritism, and inappropriate dual relationships.
  • Gifts, social media contact, transportation, financial transactions, or outside contact may be limited by agency policy and professional ethics.
  • Concerns about staff conduct may be reported through the grievance process without retaliation.

20. Accessibility, Accommodations, and Communication Needs

Skinner Legacies seeks to provide meaningful access to services. Tell staff if you need a reasonable accommodation or communication support because of a disability, literacy need, language need, sensory limitation, mobility issue, or another barrier.

Requests will be considered individually. Skinner Legacies will work to identify a reasonable and effective solution while maintaining safety, clinical appropriateness, and legal requirements. See our Reasonable Accommodations Policy for the full process.

21. Technology, Telehealth, and Electronic Communication

Skinner Legacies uses electronic systems to support scheduling, documentation, communication, and clinical operations. Electronic health information is maintained in systems selected by the agency for health care operations and privacy/security needs.

  • Telehealth may be offered when clinically appropriate and permitted. Additional consent or instructions may apply.
  • Use a private location for telehealth whenever possible.
  • Do not record a telehealth or in-person service without authorization.
  • Electronic communication should not be used for emergencies.
  • Skinner Legacies personnel may verify identity before discussing protected information by phone or electronically.
  • Clients should protect their own portal, email, and device passwords and notify the agency if they believe communications have been compromised.

Questions About This Handbook
